# Dietary Fiber Reference Guide

Dietary fiber is a type of carbohydrate that the body cannot digest. Unlike other carbs, it passes through the body without contributing calories, playing a crucial role in digestive health and satiety.

## Soluble vs. Insoluble Fiber
- **Soluble fiber**: Dissolves in water to form a gel-like substance. It helps lower blood cholesterol and glucose levels. Found in oats, peas, beans, apples, and citrus fruits.
- **Insoluble fiber**: Promotes the movement of material through the digestive system and increases stool bulk. Found in whole-wheat flour, wheat bran, nuts, beans, and vegetables (like cauliflower, green beans, and potatoes).

## Recommended Daily Intake
To support optimal bowel function and lower cardiovascular risk, standard guidelines recommend:
- **Women**: At least 21 to 25 grams of fiber per day.
- **Men**: At least 30 to 38 grams of fiber per day.
Increasing fiber intake should be done gradually alongside adequate hydration to prevent digestive discomfort.
